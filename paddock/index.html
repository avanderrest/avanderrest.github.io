    How to play

    Garage. Pick an engine, gearbox, tyres, body, suspension and one extra. Every choice shows up on the car, so a putt-putt on knobbly tyres looks nothing like a slick-shod coupe with a wing. Watch the bars: top speed and acceleration win straights, handling and grip win corners, off-road decides how much you suffer when you leave the tarmac.

    Tracks. Choose one of the eleven built-in circuits or draw your own. Set laps and how many rivals turn up. Switch the ghost on and your own quickest lap of that track runs alongside you as a faint car.

    Championship. A season is five rounds against a fixed field of rivals, for points and prize money. You start with a purse and a Putt-Putt, and every other part has to be bought out of your winnings — so a good opening round pays for the part that wins the third. Each season has its own class rules: the Village Cup will not have a V8 or a set of slicks anywhere near it, and the Putt-Putt Cup puts everybody in the same engine. The championship car is separate from your free-play one; the strip above the car in the garage says which you are working on.

    Race. Arrow keys or WASD. Up to go, down to brake or reverse, left and right to steer. Simply lifting off scrubs a lot of speed, which is usually enough for a bend. Shift fires nitro if you fitted a bottle. Yellow pads on the road are boosts.

    Drifting. Hold Space (or X) to lock the back wheels. The car pivots, leaves two black stripes and a cloud of smoke, and keeps sliding until you catch it with opposite lock. It is the quick way through a tight hairpin and the slow way through everything else.

    Slipstream and ramps. Sit right behind another car on a straight and you get towed along in its wake — the badge lights up when it is working. Wooden ramps fling you over the water; every ramp has a boost pad on the run-up, so arrive flat out and you will clear it. Land crooked and you lose most of your speed.

    Banking and loops. The outside lane of a banked bend tips up, and the slope leans on the car and pushes it down towards the apex — run high on the banking and you can carry far more speed than the flat line allows. A loop-de-loop takes whatever speed you bring to it: enough and you come out the far side, not enough and you drop off the inside near the top. Both are on the Thunderbowl, and both are in the editor.

    Handicap. Set on the tracks screen. Whoever is out in front of the field gives a few percent away and whoever is behind it gets a few percent back, easing in over a second or so. It applies to you too, so the badge in the corner tells you which way it is going. Turn it off for a straight fight.

    Editor. Paint road, gravel, mud, boost pads, ramps, banking, loops, water, trees and walls. Banking climbs away from whatever plain road it touches, so paint it as the outside lane of a bend with ordinary road beside it. A loop wants two cells of road along the direction of travel, right across the width of the track. Place a start line on the road; the road must loop back round to it. Save it and it appears in your track list.
