How it works

A Gaussian splat is a cloud of soft, coloured blobs that together look like the scene from wherever you stand. These are made from a single photo, so they only know the side the camera saw: turn too far and you see the gaps behind things. The view eases back when you push past. Some gallery scenes wrap an invented surround past that edge — painted hinterland, not a real 360 — so oil and pointillist looks have something to land on.

Gallery scenes were made from my photos with Apple's SHARP.

Quick guesses each pixel's distance with Depth Anything V2 Small and pushes it back that far. Full 3D runs SHARP itself on your graphics card through WebGPU. Its weights are Apple's, under a research-use-only licence, and come from a community copy on Hugging Face.

Look repaints the scene as you turn it. Oil paint turns every splat into a brush stroke laid along its own shape; the others are watercolour washes, pointillist dots, pencil hatching and comic ink. Picture saves the view as an image.

Drag to turn · scroll or pinch to zoom · right-drag or two fingers to move · double-click to start again. Download a scene as .splat or .ply to open it in SuperSplat or Blender.

Download SHARP?

Full 3D needs Apple's SHARP model: 2.6 GB, downloaded once and kept in this browser. It runs on your graphics card, and needs one with 8 GB or more; on a smaller one the tab may crash. The weights are for research use only.
