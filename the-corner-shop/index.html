How to play

  • You run the shop on foot. Tap the floor to walk there, or steer with WASD or the arrow keys. E or Space works whatever you are standing at.
  • Stock lives in the stockroom, back left. Walk in and tap a stack of boxes (or press E beside it) to pick one up; with a box in your arms, tap any free side of a pallet (or the stockroom) to put it down there. Carry it to a shelf and tap the shelf to stack it: its own shelf to top it up, or any empty shelf (they glow), which then becomes its shelf. A box holds what the shelf has room for; anything left over you can put back.
  • Customers walk round the shelves for what they came for. If a shelf is bare they hang about with a thought bubble for a few seconds, so a quick dash with a box can still save the sale. Then they queue at the counter.
  • The till needs you behind it. When someone is waiting there (a bell over their head), pick each item up (click it, or press and drag), move it over the scanner to ring it up, then drop it in the bag. Then click each coin they put down to take it. Their patience runs down from the moment they reach the counter, so do not get caught at the back of the shop.
  • You start with nothing. Order your first stock on the Orders tab: when the shop is empty you fetch it from the cash and carry yourself, so it is in the stockroom straight away. Carry it out to the shelves box by box, then open the door.
  • Shelves: any empty shelf takes any box, and becomes that thing's shelf. Before you open, tap a shelf with empty arms to take its things off, then stack them somewhere else or put them back in the stockroom. The bare plinths can each take another shelf for £45. Where things go matters: the far shelves are a long walk from the till.
  • Nobody says a word while they shop. You find out what they thought (too dear, sold out, wishing you stocked something) when you cash off. It stays in the Notebook tab for the morning.
  • Prices are yours to set. Push them up and some people put things back. Drop them and they grab two.
  • Orders placed before you open catch the van as you unlock the door, straight into the stockroom. Orders are taken while the shop is shut: in the evening for tomorrow morning, or in the morning before you open. Milk, bread, bananas and papers do not keep, so order what you will sell. Tomorrow's weather is in the notebook.
  • The year is four seasons of a fortnight, and the wholesaler's list changes with it: chocolate eggs in spring, sun lotion in summer, fireworks in autumn, crackers at Christmas.
  • Rent comes out every evening. Two mornings in the red and the landlord changes the locks.
