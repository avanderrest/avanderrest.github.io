How to play

  • The till is where you spend most of the day. When someone reaches the counter their shopping lands on the belt. Drag each item over the scanner to ring it up, then drop it in the bag. A tap does the same: once to scan, again to pack.
  • Once the last thing is bagged they put their money down on the counter. Click each coin to sweep it into the tin — nothing counts as takings until you have picked it up, and they will not leave until it is all gone.
  • Patience runs down while people wait. Serve them quickly and they come back and tell their friends. Let it run out and they walk off, shopping and all.
  • You start with nothing. Bare shelves, empty stockroom, and the door stays locked until there is something on a shelf. Order your first stock in on the Orders tab — when the shop is empty you fetch it from the cash and carry yourself, so you can sell it the same day.
  • Shelves only hold what you have ordered in. Click a shelf to carry stock out of the stockroom, and keep topping it up as the day goes on. While the shop is open that takes a few seconds away from the till, so pick your moment.
  • Nobody says a word while they shop. You find out what they thought — too dear, sold out, wishing you stocked something — when you cash off at the end of the day. It stays in the Notebook tab for the morning.
  • Prices are yours to set. Push them up and some people put things back. Drop them and they grab two.
  • Orders placed before you open catch the van as you unlock the door — they land in the stockroom, so you will be putting them out between customers. Order once you are open and it waits until the next morning. Milk, bread, bananas and papers do not keep, so order what you will actually sell. Tomorrow's weather is on the notebook.
  • The year is four seasons of a fortnight each, and the wholesaler's list changes with it: chocolate eggs in spring, sun lotion and ice cream in summer, fireworks and pumpkins in autumn, mince pies and crackers at Christmas. Order what you need before the van stops carrying it.
  • The hot counter — pies and sausage rolls — keeps for a day and no longer. Whatever is left at five goes in the bin, so the whole trick is ordering exactly enough.
  • Snow and a gale keep people at home, and change what the ones who come out are after: tea, beans, bread, batteries and candles rather than lollies.
  • Rent comes out every evening. Two mornings in the red and the landlord changes the locks.
  • After closing, Fill every shelf in the Shelves tab does the morning round in one go. Rearrange the shelves opens the whole wall at once: tick what you want to sell, untick what you do not, and nothing moves until you save. You can fit another shelf there too.
