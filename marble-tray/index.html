How to play

There is nothing to win. It is a tray of things that roll and slide and knock into each other.

  • Drag anything with the pointer. Let go while moving and it keeps going. Held blocks swing about the point you grabbed them by.
  • Click an item to pick it for steering, then push it about with WASD or the arrow keys. Press Tab to pick the next item, Escape or Let go to drop it.
  • Tilt the tray with the arrow keys when nothing is picked. Everything rolls downhill and the bubble in the level shows which way is up.
  • Add things from the shelf on the right. Click to drop one in, or drag it straight onto the tray.
  • Delete or Backspace takes away the item you are steering.
  • Bolt things down. The second shelf is furniture: pegs, rails, kerbs, a funnel, a chute and a cup. Pick one and click the tray to put it there; it stays armed, so you can lay a whole row of pegs. Escape puts the tool down. Click something already bolted down to pick it, drag it to move it, and drag the green knob (or press [ and ]) to turn it. This is what turns the tray into a marble run: a funnel over a chute over a cup, and everything small enough drops through.
  • The funnel sorts. Its neck is narrow enough for small marbles and ball bearings and too narrow for the rest, so a mixed handful poured in comes out separated.
  • The bumper is rubber under tension. It is the one thing on the tray that gives back more than it took.
  • Keep a tray. Name it and press Keep it and the whole layout — furniture, marbles, everything — goes into a slot you can load again later.
  • Heavy and light: glass marbles are heavy for their size, the slate puck heavier still, wood is light and cork lighter. A small marble will barely shift a plank.
  • The die turns over edge by edge as it travels rather than spinning its face about, and it never lands back on the number it just left, nor on that number's opposite.
  • The magnet drags the steel bits — ball bearings, hex nuts, the bar — across the tray from a good way off, and gets pulled about by them in return. Steel is heavy: a small bearing weighs what a big marble does.
  • Match up in the corner is the other way to play. You and an opponent each own a colour and race to sink four marbles. It starts on a 3, 2, 1 — pick the marble you want while you wait, nothing moves until the off.
  • The rings round the holes say who each one pays. A blue-ringed hole scores for you whatever drops in it, a pink-ringed one scores for the opponent, and the middle hole with the dashed ring pays whoever owns the marble. There is one of each colour on either side of the tray.
  • Sabotage. Because the ring pays, not the marble, barging one of the opponent's marbles into a blue-ringed hole is worth a point to you — and one of your own drifting into a pink ring is a point to them. Steer wide of their holes.
  • Come in slowly. A marble at full tilt rattles across a hole and out the other side — you have to ease it in. Press the opposite direction to brake, or tap the keys rather than holding them. The opponent knows this too, and the keener settings judge the run-in better.
  • Barging the opponent's marble off its line is fair play, and the opponent will do the same to you — the keener settings will shove a marble of yours straight down a hole of theirs.
  • Sound is off until you turn it on. Glass clicks, wood knocks, cork thuds, steel rings.
