How keen it is

How to play

There is nothing to win. It is a tray of things that roll and slide and knock into each other.

  • Drag anything with the pointer. Let go while moving and it keeps going. Held blocks swing about the point you grabbed them by.
  • Tilt the tray with WASD or the arrow keys and everything rolls downhill for as long as you hold. The bubble under the tray shows which way is down.
  • Lock the tilt to keep the tray leaning without holding anything: press Shift and an arrow, or use the pad of arrows under the tray. Press it again to take that lean off, T or the dot in the middle to level up. Gentle, Normal, Steep sets how hard the slope pulls. A locked tilt is what makes a marble run actually run.
  • Steer is a switch, off to begin with. Turn it on and clicking a thing picks it up for the keys — then WASD or the arrows push that one thing about instead of tilting the tray. Tab picks the next one, Escape or Let go drops it, Delete takes it away. Any locked tilt keeps working underneath.
  • Rotate is a switch too. With it on, dragging anything bolted down swings it round its middle rather than sliding it; with it off, dragging moves it. [ and ] nudge the angle either way. Loose things are left alone while Rotate is on, so you can turn a rail in a tray full of marbles.
  • Add things from the shelf on the right. The tabs sort it: marbles, odds and ends, steel, and the furniture you bolt down. Click to drop one in, or drag it straight onto the tray.
  • Bolt things down. The last tab is furniture: pegs, rails, kerbs, a funnel, a chute and a cup. Pick one and click the tray to put it there; it stays armed, so you can lay a whole row of pegs. Escape, or moving off that tab, puts the tool down. This is what turns the tray into a marble run: a funnel over a chute over a cup, a tilt locked towards you, and everything small enough drops through.
  • Strings. The same tab has a String. Pick it, click a thing to tie one end to it, then click the tray to tack the other end down — the thing hangs off that tack and swings when the tray tips. A long string hangs loose, a short one holds the thing up taut; click a string and Delete / Backspace takes it away and the knot comes loose.
  • Delete or Backspace takes away whatever is picked — the fixture you selected, or the thing you are steering.
  • The funnel sorts. Its neck is narrow enough for small marbles and ball bearings and too narrow for the rest, so a mixed handful poured in comes out separated.
  • The bumper is rubber under tension. It is the one thing on the tray that gives back more than it took.
  • Keep a tray. Name it and press Keep it and the whole layout — furniture, marbles, everything — goes into a slot you can load again later.
  • Heavy and light: glass marbles are heavy for their size, the slate puck heavier still, wood is light and cork lighter. A small marble will barely shift a plank.
  • The die turns over edge by edge as it travels rather than spinning its face about, and it never lands back on the number it just left, nor on that number's opposite.
  • The magnet drags the steel bits — ball bearings, hex nuts, the bar — across the tray from a good way off, and gets pulled about by them in return. Steel is heavy: a small bearing weighs what a big marble does.
  • Match up in the corner is the other way to play. Twelve marbles lie on the tray and you each drive a shooter — the big one in your colour. A shooter is too fat to fit down a hole, so you cannot put yourself in: the only way to score is to knock a marble in with it.
  • Set your shooter down on the 3, 2, 1. Click anywhere in your half of the tray and it goes there; nothing moves until the off, and from then on it is yours where you left it.
  • The rings round the holes say who each one pays. A blue-ringed hole scores for you whatever drops in it, a pink-ringed one scores for the opponent, and the middle hole with the dashed ring pays whoever owns the marble.
  • Either colour will do. Because the ring pays and not the marble, a pink marble down a blue hole is worth exactly as much as a blue one — and the opponent is just as happy putting yours down a pink hole, which costs you the marble as well as the point.
  • All twelve go in and the bigger half wins. The only early finish is a lead that the marbles still on the tray cannot make up.
  • Come in slowly. A marble shoved at full tilt rattles across a hole and out the other side — you have to ease it in. Tap the keys rather than leaning on them, or press the opposite direction to pull the shooter off it and let the marble coast. The opponent knows this too, and the keener settings judge the run-in better.
  • Two players. Under Opponent, swap the computer for a friend: player one drives the sky shooter on WASD, player two the rose shooter on the arrows, both on the one keyboard.
  • Sound is off until you turn it on. Glass clicks, wood knocks, cork thuds, steel rings.
