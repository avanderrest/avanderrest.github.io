How to play

Three games on the one tray, picked at the top: a match, a maze and bowls.

Driving a shooter

  • In a match and in a maze you drive a shooter, the big marble, with WASD or the arrow keys — or the pad under the panels on a phone. A shooter is too fat to fit down a hole, so you cannot put yourself in: the only way to score is to knock a marble in with it.
  • How hard you hit is how long you hold. A key winds the shooter up rather than starting it at full pelt: a tap is a gentle nudge, and it takes about a second of holding to reach full speed.
  • Come in slowly. A marble shoved at full tilt rattles across a hole and out the other side — you have to ease it in. Tap the keys rather than leaning on them, or press the opposite direction to pull the shooter off it and let the marble coast.
  • Shift is the brake. Held on its own it pulls a shooter that is running away back down to a crawl; held with a direction it keeps the push gentle however long you lean on it.

Match

  • Twelve marbles lie on the tray and you each drive a shooter in your colour. Nothing moves until you press Play in the middle of the tray; then set your shooter down on the 3, 2, 1: click anywhere in your half of the tray and it goes there.
  • The rings round the holes say who each one pays. A blue-ringed hole scores for you whatever drops in it, a pink-ringed one scores for the opponent, and the middle hole with the dashed ring pays whoever owns the marble. So a pink marble down a blue hole is worth exactly as much as a blue one.
  • All twelve go in and the bigger half wins. The only early finish is a lead that the marbles still on the tray cannot make up.
  • Nothing can get stuck. If a marble settles against the rim and nothing has dropped for a while the tray shakes itself, and after three shakes the marble is set down in the middle.
  • Two players. Under Opponent, swap the computer for a friend: player one drives sky on WASD, player two rose on the arrows. Each has their own brake: left Shift for player one, right Shift for player two.

Maze

  • Push the little amber marble through the maze and into the brass hole at the far end, using the big one. You can only push, never pull, so think about which side of it you need to be on before you come to a turning.
  • Turn a corner by striking the little marble a glancing blow: hit it from the inside of the bend and the outer wall will turn it for you.
  • Blind ends are where the trouble is. Some have a hole in them that the little marble will drop down — it comes back to the start if it does — and a little marble pushed right to the end of one is somewhere the big one cannot get behind. R, or Back to the start, puts both marbles back at the mouth of the maze with the clock still running.
  • Eight mazes, each a little bigger than the last; getting through one opens the next. In the last ones some corridors are too narrow for the big marble to get past the little one, so the little one has to lead the way.

Bowls

  • Four big marbles a side, rolled from the dark mat on the left at the rings painted on the far end of the cloth, as at curling. Turns go back and forth until all eight are down.
  • Press anywhere and pull back to aim, like a catapult: the marble goes the opposite way to your pull, and the further you pull the harder it goes. The ring shows where it would stop if nothing were in the way. Tap the mat first to move your marble up or down it.
  • A marble that stops short of the dashed line is dead and comes off. Anything else is in play — knock theirs out of the rings, nudge your own in, it all counts.
  • At the end of each end, whoever lies nearest the middle of the rings scores one for every marble of theirs in the rings nearer than the other side's best. A marble that is not touching the outer ring does not count. They go first in the next end. First to five wins.
  • The computer bowls by trying its shots out first, so the keener it is, the more likely it is to find the one that knocks yours away.
  • Sound is off until you turn it on. Glass clicks on glass and knocks on wood.
