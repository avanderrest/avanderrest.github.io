Physics Playground

Getting the sand out…

How to play

Seven toys, each with its own physics. Pick one from the bar at the top.

  • Gummy bears — grab a bear anywhere and pull: an arm or a leg stretches out thin and snaps back. Fling them, pile them up, thump the table. Tap empty space to drop in a new bear. Jiggle runs from gummy to jelly.
  • Lava — drag through the blobs to slice them; surface tension pulls them back together. Pour adds more. With Lamp heat on, the bottom heats the lava till it rises and the top cools it till it sinks.
  • Kinetic sand — 50,000 grains in a tray. Pick a shape and tap the sand to drop it; heavy ones sink deeper. Tap a shape to lift it out and see its print. Poke lets you press and draw in the sand with a finger. Turn the tray with ⟲ ⟳, or drag with two fingers (right-drag with a mouse); pinch or scroll to zoom. Hold is how firmly the sand keeps its shape. Swing hangs a sand pendulum over the tray: drag the funnel aside and let go, with a flick for an ellipse, and it draws in coloured sand. Pattern changes how its two swings beat against each other.
  • Chaos pendulum — a double pendulum. Drag either bob to set it, then let go. The faint ghosts start a thousandth of a radian apart and soon go their own way: the lower graph is how far apart, on a log scale. The square is the two angles plotted against each other.
  • Marbled ink — ebru, Turkish paper marbling. Tap the water to drop ink, and hold to let the drop spread; each new drop pushes the ones under it into rings. Then draw through it with the Stylus or the Comb (a pen works well, and pressing harder is a wider stylus). Comb across draws a comb over the whole tray, a different way each time.
  • Soap bubbles — wave the wand through the air: a slow sweep blows one big bubble, a quick one a stream of small ones. Hold the wand on its stand, press the space bar, or blow into the microphone to blow bubbles there. Bubbles that touch stick into foam. Tap one to pop it.
  • Curtain — two cloth curtains on a rail. Grab pulls the cloth, and pulled too hard it tears; grab a ring at the top to draw a curtain open or shut. Cut slices through it. The breeze comes in at the window.
