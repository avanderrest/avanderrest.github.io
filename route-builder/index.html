      How to play

      The town and the toy box

      • The mat starts as empty grass. Houses go up a few at a time where the town wants to grow — and faster around your stations.
      • There's no money. You have a toy box of stations, track pieces, bridges, trains and carriages, and every few houses the town adds more to it.
      • Houses nobody serves glow orange, then red as they grow — that's where the next station or line should go. A station with no track through it glows too.
      • Anything you pull up goes back in the toy box whole.

      Building

      • Station (S): click the grass to place one. The ring shows how many houses would feed it — more houses, more passengers. Colours take turns — red, yellow, blue, green, then round again — and the ghost’s roof shows which one is next.
      • Click a station that's already placed to upgrade it: a wooden platform goes round it with room for more people, and trains load faster. The first upgrade takes 1 station from the toy box, the second takes 2. Hover first to see the new size.
      • Track (T): click a station, then click the one to join it to — keep clicking to carry the line on. Or drag from a station across more stations in one go. Start from the end of a line to extend it. Every 70 or so of mat takes a piece.
      • Bridges aren't placed by hand: track that crosses the stream somewhere new uses one up from the toy box. Pull the track up and the bridge comes down with it — it doesn't go back in the toy box.
      • Train (R): click some track to put a train on it; click a train to hitch a carriage. A train runs over all the track it’s joined to.
      • Where track branches at a station, the points flip every time a train goes through, so trains take each way in turn. The track shows how they’re set: next to a junction, the way the next train through won’t take is painted red. At the end of the track trains turn round.
      • Remove (X): click a train, a run of track or an unused station to put it back — hovering track outlines the whole run a click would pull up. A station can’t go on top of track — pull the track up first.

      Keeping it running

      • Every peg wants a station of its own colour — any station of that colour will do. People ride a train while it’s heading their way, and get off to change where it turns off.
      • A station piling up past its platform drains Happiness. Redraw the network before it runs out.
      • Houses with no station close by still send people: they walk to the nearest one, which takes a while, and arrive impatient (ringed orange). Walkers crowd a platform more, and once they've waited too long (ringed red) they drain Happiness on their own.
      • Survival never ends on its own — it runs until the town gets too far ahead of your network. Calm mode never fails — build at your own pace.

      Getting around

      • With no tool picked, click a station — or its name in the Stations list — to see who uses it: the ring of houses close enough to feed it, those houses lit up in gold, and the ones further out that walk in ringed in orange. Click the grass or press Esc to put it away.
      • Scroll or pinch to zoom; drag open grass to pan. Fit view snaps back to the whole town.
      • Space pauses, 1 2 3 set the speed, Esc opens the menu.
