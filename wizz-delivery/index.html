How to play

Your job. You are a food courier. Shift lasts five minutes. Park the car up next to a restaurant and it shows you what it has on the board — which house the order is heading to, how far it is, and what it pays. Two restaurants that sit near each other let you stack two bags before you turn the key.

Accepting. Hit Accept on one row, or a row on each of a handful of nearby restaurants. Everything you accept goes into the En route list, and every one of them starts a countdown before you have left the car park.

Delivery. Drive to the house. The nearest un-delivered order is marked on the map and in the top corner, and you can click any row to retarget. Reach the door and the fare is settled on the spot: fast — before a generous mark — and you take the fare with a tip; on time and you take the fare; late and the kitchen fines you instead. You deliver to every house you happen to reach, not just the one you are tracking.

The city. WASD or the arrow keys; up to go, down to brake and reverse, left and right to steer. Tarmac is fast, pavement and parks are not, and buildings and trees stop you dead — so the quick line through a block is a choice, not a given. Offers that nobody accepts quietly lapse, so a greedy count on the board is another kind of deadline.

Scoring. You start each shift with a $25 float. Earned is the total you have put on the books so far this shift — fares plus tips, fines taken off — and your best shift is kept on this machine.
